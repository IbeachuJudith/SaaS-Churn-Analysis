# HR-Attrition-Analysis
**Business Problem**

Employee attrition is one of the most pressing challenges facing HR teams. Every departure increases hiring and training costs, disrupts productivity, and lowers the morale of the people who stay. Without a clear picture of why employees leave, organizations end up reacting to turnover instead of preventing it.

**This project set out to answer three questions:**

Where is attrition most concentrated across job roles, career levels, and demographics?
Why are employees leaving, and how do compensation, growth opportunities, travel, and workload contribute?
What can the company do to improve retention?

**Tools Used**
Power Query: data cleaning and transformation
Excel: calculated fields and attrition rate metrics
PivotTables: summarizing and comparing groups
Charts: visualizing attrition patterns.

Dataset

The dataset contains demographic, compensation, and job-related details for employees, including job role, age, salary level, career level, travel frequency, and workload.

**Data Preparation**
1. Cleaned and transformed the data in Power Query, handling null values and restructuring columns.
2. Created calculated fields in Excel to measure attrition rates by job role, age, and salary level.
3. Built PivotTables for quick analysis and comparison across groups.

**Analysis Focus**
1. Attrition rates by job role
2. Salary level vs. attrition likelihood
3. Career level and growth opportunities
4. Impact of travel frequency and workload on retention

**Key Insights**
1. Sales Representatives had the highest attrition (50%), linked to low pay and limited advancement opportunities.
2. Entry-level employees left at higher rates despite high job satisfaction, pointing to unmet career development expectations.
3. Frequent travelers had higher attrition, suggesting travel demands should be communicated more clearly during hiring.
4. Mid-level roles had lower attrition, showing retention improves significantly once employees advance.

**Recommendations**
1. Launch career development programs for entry-level staff.
2. Review compensation and promotion policies for Sales Representatives.
3. Clarify travel expectations during recruitment.
4. Build pathways for internal mobility so employees can grow without leaving.
